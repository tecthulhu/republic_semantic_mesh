## A correction to the instruction's premise

The order says to build "per its ratified acceptance criteria (ratified in the
consolidation ceremony, DEC-0009)". SPEC-0116 and SPEC-0117 were **not** ratified
there — DEC-0009 ratified the five sets of STORY-0013 through STORY-0017, and
STORY-0010 was not among them. Both criteria stood `proposed` when this shipment
arrived.

This matters beyond bookkeeping. Building against criteria no floor act has touched is
the condition SPEC-0126's pin exists to make visible, and proceeding on the assumption
that a touch had happened would have been the same class of error the pin catches. So
this decision supplies the touch the instruction assumed, which is why a ceremony rides
with the build.

## What the primitive is

A record form for *a human examined this and rendered a verdict*, and nothing more.

**Record.** `EVID-human-<claim>-<t>.json` in the Acta: the claim, the human's identity,
a pass/fail verdict, a note, and the corpus digest examined. **Storage** is the same
append-only Acta every other control writes to — no new store. **Attribution** is the
`checker` field, supplied explicitly and never derived.

**It carries no authority, and that is the point.** Floor-touch is authorization — a
merge, a Duo approval, *I permit this*. Human evidence is examination — *I looked, and
here is my verdict*. The corpus has kept these as cousins through STORY-0014's pin and
the ferry's machine/principal split, and collapsing them here would undo both. A record
`authorized_by: null`; its authority is the signing commit that lands it, exactly as a
ceremony's is. No new authority mechanism is invented.

**Emission has no automated path**, which is what stops `check: human` becoming a
rubber stamp with better provenance. The emitter refuses a `check: machine` claim,
refuses an absent or empty checker with no default and no derivation from git config,
and refuses a claim resolving to nothing.

**Staleness is content-addressed, not scheduled.** A record is true-at-T against the
digest it names (ONT-014); when the corpus moves, the record stops covering the claim
and the claim returns to unevidenced. A fresh verdict at the new digest restores it —
staleness is not death. No expiry timer, no calendar, nothing to remember.

## What the control asserts, and what it cannot

CTRL-0012 asserts the *record exists*, names a human, and is current. It does not grade
the judgment inside — that is the human's, and a control claiming to verify it would be
the overclaim SPEC-0135's own conformance clause forbids. SPEC-0135 is the first claim
bound by it, closing the gap DEC-0008 recorded openly rather than dressing as
machine-checked.

## What ratifying moves, and the one number that does not move as far as it could

- Five atoms `proposed → ratified`; the reconcile activates CTRL-0012 (its
  implementation resolves), RULE-0101, and SPEC-0135 with it.
- `unbound_claims` **12 → 11**. SPEC-0135 leaves; SPEC-0116 and SPEC-0117 arrive
  ratified *and bound*, so they add nothing to the meter.

  This nearly went wrong, and the correction is worth recording. The first cut of this
  decision ratified SPEC-0116 and SPEC-0117 with no binding rules — and an earlier
  draft of this very paragraph asserted RULE-0078 and RULE-0079 bound them, which was
  false. The reconcile put `unbound_claims` at **13**: one claim left the meter and two
  arrived. That is precisely the arithmetic DEC-0009 was careful about six days ago
  when it ratified nine claims *and* their nine rules in one act, reproduced here by
  ratifying claims whose control existed while their rules did not. RULE-0102 and
  RULE-0103 are the fix, and the meter is what caught it.

**The eleven are deliberately not bound here, and the reason is the point.** Binding
them to CTRL-0012 is now possible and would take `unbound_claims` to zero this
afternoon. SPEC-0117 ties reaching zero to something else: *the eleven are evidenced by
the owner in one reviewing pass*. Reaching zero by binding alone would satisfy the
number while leaving the criterion untouched — the meter reading zero because eleven
claims acquired a control, not because anyone examined them.

So the binding waits for the examination it is supposed to record. The tool is built and
refuses to invent a checker; the eleven verdicts are the owner's to render, and
`unbound_claims` reaches zero when they are rendered rather than when a rule is written.
